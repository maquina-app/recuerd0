# The workspace blueprint

Every new recuerd0 account starts with a Start Here workspace that is already shaped: a map, a continuation brief, and a decisions index. This page is the reasoning behind that shape — what each piece is for, when to grow past it, and how to leave it behind entirely.

The stance throughout is convention over configuration. The skeleton carries you fast at the start; nothing about it is enforced, and every piece of it can be deleted.

## The shape

**_MAP** is the front door. A session — yours or an AI tool's — reads it first and knows where everything lives. Two rules make a map work: every memory stays reachable within two hops of it, and every line is written in the words someone would actually ask with. "Where do the images live" routes a cold session; "storage architecture" alone may not. When you add a memory, add its line.

**Continuation Brief** carries state between sessions: where things stand, what's open, what to pick up next. Rewrite it when a session ends and save it as a new version — the version history becomes your session log without any extra ceremony.

**_INDEX — Decisions** holds one line per locked decision, numbered D001, D002, and so on. Each decision gets its own memory: what you chose, why, what you rejected. Decisions don't get edited afterward — a changed mind is a new decision that points back at the old one. The index means any decision is two hops from the map.

**Hubs** are routing tables for crowded territory: one line of judgment per entry, pointing at the memory with the detail. Hubs are not seeded — you grow into them. Keep the map flat until around twenty memories; when a cluster forms, promote it behind a hub and give the map one line pointing there.

## Finding things

Search in recuerd0 is keyword search — fast and literal. It is at its best with exact names and identifiers: a decision number, a function name, a phrase you know is in the text. It is not the tool for questions phrased in your own words; the map is. That division of labor is deliberate: the map encodes the judgment that keyword search doesn't have, and writing map lines in asking-vocabulary is what makes it pay off. Semantic search is on the roadmap as an opt-in addition; the map-first habit will still be the backbone when it lands.

## When your workspace grows

A few patterns that earn their keep on larger workspaces, none of them needed early:

- **Supersession.** When a new decision replaces an old one, mark the old memory as superseded in its body and point both at each other. The trail matters more than tidiness.
- **Versioning by type.** Briefs get a version every session; decisions never change; working documents version at milestones and get edited freely in between.
- **Agent write rules.** If AI tools write to your workspace, decide when they may: a simple, durable rule is that an agent drafts and a person confirms before anything is filed.
- **Structural edits in two passes.** Add content memories first, then update the map or hub that routes to them. A map line pointing at nothing is worse than a memory waiting for its line.

## Getting out of the way

The blueprint is a starting shape, not a system requirement. Blank workspaces are first-class. Flat workspaces are fine at any size if they work for you. Rename the pieces, keep only the map, or delete everything and organize your own way — the product does not treat the skeleton specially. The only conviction underneath it all is curation over accumulation: a small set of memories you trust beats a large pile you don't.
